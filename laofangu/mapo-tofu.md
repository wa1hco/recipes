# Mapo Tofu (麻婆豆腐)

Xu Meng’s restaurant version — firm tofu, not silken.

**Source:** 老饭骨 / LaoFanGu, Xu Meng (徐萌), with Sun Lixin (孙立新, “Er Bo”)  
**Watch:** [youtube.com/watch?v=19CjUyxZVgM](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=19CjUyxZVgM) (2026-03-22, 4:47)

Shop’s top seller. They describe it at the end as **numbing, spicy, salty, savory, slightly sweet**.

The video does not overlay gram weights. Amounts below are a home-wok scale for **one block (~400 g) of firm tofu**. Where they only said “a little,” that is noted.

## Ingredients

**Tofu**

- 400 g firm tofu (韧豆腐) — not silken. Cube it. They want bounce, so it does not fall apart.

**Blanching water**

- Water to cover
- A little dark soy (老抽) — instead of salt, for a base flavor and better color (not stark white)

**Wok oils**

- Rapeseed oil (菜籽油), enough to coat the wok (~2 Tbsp)
- Chili oil (红油), a little at the start and a little more at the end

**Aromatics and meat**

- 80–100 g minced beef
- Minced garlic
- Minced ginger
- A little fermented black beans / douchi (豆豉) — some people skip this; they add it and call it a must
- Pixian chili bean paste / doubanjiang (豆瓣酱) — about 1–1½ Tbsp; this is the salt
- A splash of yellow rice wine — they used 20-year Nüerhong (女儿红); Shaoxing works

**Chili and pepper**

- Chili powder, two grinds mixed: one fine, one coarse (~1 tsp each)
- Sichuan pepper powder (花椒面) — about ½ tsp. Add now, on low heat. Do not wait until plating. Do not overcook or it tastes burnt.

**Liquid and seasoning**

- Hot water to cover (stock if you have it)
- Chicken essence / chicken powder (鸡精)
- Chicken juice / concentrate (鸡汁)
- A pinch of MSG (味精)
- A little sugar (糖)
- Water-starch slurry (水淀粉) — 1 tsp cornstarch + 1 Tbsp water
- Scallion greens (葱花), to finish

**Do not add**

- Salt
- Soy sauce in the wok (dark soy is only in the blanching water). Extra soy muddies the color; the doubanjiang is already salty enough.

## Cooking

1. Cube the firm tofu. Put on a pot of water. Add a little dark soy. Slide in the tofu. When it boils, cook about **1 minute**. Drain. Do not rinse.
2. Heat the wok. Rapeseed oil, then a little chili oil — two oils.
3. Fry the minced beef until it is fragrant and a bit crispy. If the wok was not oiled first, it may stick; keep frying.
4. Garlic and ginger in with the beef. Fry together.
5. A little douchi. Then doubanjiang. Splash in the yellow wine.
6. Two chili powders (fine + coarse), mixed. Then Sichuan pepper powder, still in the wok. **Low heat** to bloom the aroma. Do not let the pepper powder scorch.
7. Add water or stock. Bring it together.
8. Season: chicken essence, chicken juice, a little MSG, a little sugar. Taste. No salt, no extra soy.
9. Tofu back in. Simmer so it takes on the sauce.
10. Water-starch slurry to a glossy, clingy sauce — they wait until it looks “to that thickness.”
11. Finish with a little chili oil for color. Scallion greens. Serve with rice.

## The three points they say to nail

1. **Firm tofu, not silken.** Tender, slick, with bounce; it does not shatter when you stir. Traditional mapo uses soft tofu, which falls apart.
2. **Beef fried until crispy.** That crunch is the texture they want.
3. **Sichuan pepper powder — don’t overcook it.** Too much heat and it tastes burnt. Chili powder should be just enough: a light numbing aroma, no harsh bitterness.

Also: **no salt, no soy in the stir-fry.** Chili paste and the other seasonings already have enough salt.
