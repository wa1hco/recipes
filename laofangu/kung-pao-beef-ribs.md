# Kung Pao Beef Ribs (宫保牛肋)

Western sear, Chinese kung pao sauce.

**Source:** 老饭骨 / LaoFanGu, Xu Meng (徐萌), with Sun Lixin (孙立新, “Er Bo”) and Du Guangzhen (杜广贞)  
**Watch:** [youtube.com/watch?v=SpoFxXHZ_Gs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SpoFxXHZ_Gs) (2026-07-14, 10:25)

Xu Meng sears beef rib chunks Western-style (red wine, salt, black pepper), then finishes them in a kung pao sauce. The sauce mixes the old “small lizhi” profile with a modern big sweet-and-sour. The sauce grams are theirs, spoken on camera.

## Ingredients

**Beef**

- ~500 g (1 jin; they weighed a little over) beef rib strips (牛肋条), cut in large chunks
- Red wine, a splash (or Shaoxing / yellow wine) — aroma and to tenderize
- Salt, a little — base flavor only
- MSG, a pinch (in the marinade, not in the sauce)
- Black pepper, enough to actually taste

**Kung pao sauce** (they say this batch does 3–4 portions)

- Sugar 65 g
- Salt 3 g
- Rice vinegar 75 g
- Light soy 2.5 g
- Dark soy (冰糖老抽) 4.5 g

**Wok**

- Thin kung pao scallions, cut in sections (or the traditional scored-and-opened scallion)
- Dried chilies
- Sichuan pepper (花椒) and mala pepper (麻椒) — one numbing, one fragrant
- Garlic slices
- Ginger slices
- Roasted peanuts
- Cashews (optional — they say one kind of nut is enough)
- Water-starch slurry, added a little at a time
- Chili oil (红油)
- Vinegar for the wok edge at the end

## Cooking

1. Cut the rib strips into large chunks. This is a hard sear, not a stew.
2. **Marinate, Western style.** Red wine (or yellow wine), a little salt, MSG, black pepper. Mix. This is the base; the kung pao sauce later must not be too salty.
3. Mix the sauce in a bowl: sugar 65 g, salt 3 g, rice vinegar 75 g, soy 2.5 g, dark soy 4.5 g.
4. Oil-slick the wok. Heat, pour all the oil out. You want a film, not a pool.
5. **Sear the beef on the highest heat**, in two batches so the wok stays hot. Don’t move it. Fast, hard sear to lock the juices — the opposite of kung pao chicken, which is velveted in cooler oil. Outside should pick up a little char; doneness about **4–5**. The wok should stay dry. If juices run, you seared too slow and it will be tough.
6. **Sauce, lowest flame.** Oil must not be hot. Sichuan pepper + mala pepper, then dried chilies, low heat until they color. Garlic and ginger slices, still low, until fragrant.
7. Turn the heat up. Pour in about half the sauce (they had a large batch). **Starch slurry a little at a time** — dump it and it clumps.
8. Beef back in. Scallion sections, peanuts (and cashews if you want). Chili oil. **Vinegar on the wok edge** at the end — aroma you can smell more than taste.

## The points they say to nail

1. **Hot sear, don’t fuss with it.** Lock the juices. Two batches if one drop in wok heat. Dry pan = you did it.
2. **Sauce ratio.** Sugar 65 / vinegar 75. Vinegar de-fishes then mostly cooks off; what’s left is balanced, not sour, not candy-sweet. Wok-edge vinegar is perfume, not a sour hit.
3. **Peppers and chilies on low heat** until fragrant. High heat burns them.
4. **Don’t salt the sauce hard** — the marinade already seasoned the beef, and they put MSG in the meat, not the bowl.
